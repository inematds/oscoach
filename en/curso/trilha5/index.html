🧾 Tax & Bookkeeping OS

The knowledge-heavy domain (~34%): a compendium of laws, a pipeline for bank statement PDFs, monthly review, and the skeptical reviewer who chews you out before the accountant does.

What is:

The author’s distribution: 34% context, 18% tools, 16% skills, 14% rules, 10% agents, 8% identity.

Why learn:

Shows in practice that the biggest part here is context — not agents.

Key concepts:

Context dominates · minimal identity · uneven pie chart.

What is:

Scan “the worst audits” + legislation from several provinces to create a compendium for your tax system.

Why learn:

Reversing the worst-case scenario prevents the classification error that turns into an audit.

Key concepts:

Audit pains · compendium · gray area vs. black and white.

What is:

PDF statements become text/markdown in a folder pipeline to save context window space.

Why learn:

Raw PDFs blow through the context window; converting and synthesizing them is what makes review affordable.

Key concepts:

raw → converted → synthesized · token savings.

What is:

The skill that takes the statement, categorizes it in a ledger, and prepares it to send to the accountant + spending alerts.

Why learn:

It's the domain's most repeated task — the number one candidate to become a skill.

Key concepts:

Categorized ledger · alerts · monthly cadence.

What is:

Never mix personal and business; block PII (SIN, account); convert everything to one currency (CAD).

Why learn:

Money and sensitive data call for deterministic hooks, not “soft” rules.

Key concepts:

PII guard · single currency · account separation.

What is:

A tax strategist and a skeptical reviewer who "chews you out before the accountant does".

Why learn:

Agents are few here (~10%) — they’re only used to judge and review.

Key concepts:

Adversarial reviewer · review gate · judgment.

What is:

A ready-to-use prompt for Claude Code to build the pipeline for converting PDF statements.

Why learn:

It’s the copy-run that brings the raw → converted → synthesized pipeline to life.

Key concepts:

Copy · run · verify the output.

📈 Sales OS

The domain where tools and skills matter most: lead scrapers, custom enrichment, qualification with friction, and anti-spray-and-pray rules.

What is:

30% context, 20% tools, 16% skills, 14% agents, 12% rules, 8% identity.

Why learn:

Compared to Tax, the stack is more “tightly coupled”: tool and context lead together.

Key concepts:

Tools + context at the top · same shape, new proportions.

What is:

Cold email structures, the ICP (ideal customer profile), and lead demographics stored as context.

Why learn:

Without a clear ICP, the scraper collects junk and you burn your reputation.

Key concepts:

ICP · cold email · won/lost deals.

What is:

Apify Actors (LinkedIn, Google Maps) become a lead database that enters the CRM via CLI.

Why learn:

It's the tools slice (~20%) — the largest after context.

Key concepts:

Apify actors · lead database · CRM seamless.

What is:

Your own enrichment (instead of paying for Clay) + humanizers that remove the "AI smell" from the copy.

Why learn:

Internalizing the enrichment cuts costs and gives you control over quality.

Key concepts:

Custom enrichment · humanize copy · human speak.

What is:

Friction points in the skill that force you to rescan the lead until you confirm it’s good.

Why learn:

Don't spend 100 calls on 50 bad leads — friction filters them before the effort.

Key concepts:

Intentional friction · verification loop · quality > volume.

What is:

SDR + orchestrator + human review; rules: no spray-and-pray, respect opt-outs, brand voice.

Why learn:

Spamming burns your domain and brand; an opt-out becomes a hook that immediately removes the contact from the CRM.

Key concepts:

SDR · do not contact · don’t be pushy.

What is:

A ready-to-use prompt that qualifies leads against the ICP with friction points and a Yes/No/Maybe verdict.

Why learn:

It’s the copy-run that keeps you from burning calls on cold leads.

Key concepts:

Copy · run · check the verdict.

🎧 Support OS

Aggregated tickets become playbooks; triage, drafting, and escalation skills; a skeptical reviewer before the customer; and the hook that requires citing the policy source.

What is:

A structure similar to sales: context (tickets) + skills (triage/draft) do the heavy lifting.

Why learn:

Shows how the same format works for a customer service domain.

Key concepts:

Tickets as context · response skills · escalation.

What is:

Pull Zendesk/HubSpot tickets + help docs; a long thread = a proxy for a complex case.

Why learn:

The longest threads hide the cases most worth turning into playbooks.

Key concepts:

Aggregate tickets · long thread = complexity · help docs.

What is:

Synthesize long threads into nuggets: "how to handle the client who always brings up X."

Why learn:

A playbook turns a rare case into a repeatable, consistent response.

Key concepts:

Nuggets · edge cases · escalation path.

What is:

Email triage, draft replies, and rules for when/how to escalate.

Why learn:

Reduces time spent on repetitive questions without sacrificing quality.

Key concepts:

Triage · draft reply · escalation path.

What is:

A support lead who closes tickets + a skeptical reviewer who checks the response before it goes out.

Why learn:

The review gate prevents an incorrect response from reaching the customer.

Key concepts:

Support lead · devil's advocate · double-check.

What is:

No promises outside the playbook; a hook that blocks policy responses without a link; the junior only reads the CRM.

Why learn:

An improper promise becomes a legal risk — so it becomes a hook, not a soft rule.

Key concepts:

Cite a source (hook) · junior read-only · no overpromising.

What is:

A ready-to-use prompt that classifies tickets by urgency/topic and decides whether to respond, escalate, or ask for more information.

Why learn:

It’s the copy-run that organizes the queue before any response.

Key concepts:

Copy · run · check the classification.

🎬 Content OS

Competitor reverse engineering, a persona-based headline factory, repurposing engines, and the no-hype rule that prohibits "insane".

What is:

A domain with a better balance of context, skills, and rules.

Why learn:

Shows a case where no single layer dominates.

Key concepts:

Balance · creative skills · voice rules.

What is:

Analyze the top 10 videos in the niche: save the pacing, angle, and storyline as context.

Why learn:

Competitors' patterns inform your own content.

Key concepts:

Top 10 · patterns · trend.

What is:

Hundreds of titles with AB tests + audience personas giving feedback.

Why learn:

It's the domain's star skill — generates and tests at scale.

Key concepts:

AB test · personas · scale.

What is:

Reuse content across multiple formats and channels.

Why learn:

Multiplies the reach of every piece you produce.

Key concepts:

Repurpose · multichannel · thumbnail briefs.

What is:

Producer (idea→publication) + orchestrator (writing/editing via Descript CLI).

Why learn:

Delivers a care package ready for the human editor.

Key concepts:

Producer · orchestrator · care package.

What is:

Ban hype/overpromising (e.g., "insane") and require a 30–40s anti-formula hook.

Why learn:

Protects the brand voice from falling into AI clichés.

Key concepts:

No-hype · 30–40s hook · anti-cliché.

What is:

A ready-to-use prompt that generates titles and tests them against audience personas.

Why learn:

It’s the copy-run that brings the title factory to life.

Key concepts:

Copy · run · choose the best title.

💼 Consulting OS

One folder per client, playbooks + global wiki, and the adversarial review gate in the client's voice—sometimes with multiple reviewers.

What is:

Context still matters, but rules & hooks carry more weight than usual.

Why learn:

Each client requires its own boundaries — hence the weight of the rules.

Key concepts:

Heavy rules · per-client context.

What is:

Each client is a folder; CLAUDE.md maps “when I mention X, it’s this folder.”

Why learn:

Organizes scattered information into clear drawers.

Key concepts:

Folder per client · mapping in the master file.

What is:

Client playbooks that roll up into a global playbook; a wiki that triages meetings.

Why learn:

Every business has patterns—the wiki enriches meeting prep.

Key concepts:

Client playbook · wiki · standards.

What is:

Pre-call prep, proposal/SOW, and audit — always tailored to the objective, never generic.

Why learn:

A generic audit won’t work for consulting; you need the client’s context.

Key concepts:

Nuanced pre-call · SOW · audit.

What is:

An agent named after the contact, primed with synthesized meeting notes, reviews how the client would react.

Why learn:

Anticipates objections before sending the brief—sometimes it takes several reviewers.

Key concepts:

Adversarial reviewer · customer’s voice · objections.

What is:

QuickBooks, ledgers, Stripe links in proposals (without PandaDoc), and checking calendar buffer time.

Why learn:

The right connections eliminate billing and scheduling friction.

Key concepts:

Stripe link · scheduling buffer · ledgers.

What is:

A ready-to-use prompt that creates the reviewer who responds like the client, based on the synthesized meetings.

Why learn:

It’s the copy-run that brings the adversarial review gate to life.

Key concepts:

Copy · run · review the brief.

🗽 Freedom OS & Production Standards

Local models for sensitive data, backup across 3 fronts, context rot and the /tldr that updates CLAUDE.md on its own.

What is:

Run local models for sensitive information (e.g., birth certificate) — hardware investment.

Why learn:

Some data should never go into a cloud context.

Key concepts:

Local · hardware · sensitive data.

What is:

The choice between closed, open, and a combination of both models.

Why learn:

The hybrid balances power and privacy.

Key concepts:

Closed · open · hybrid.

What is:

Private GitHub (with .gitignore for sensitive files) + SSD + cloud.

Why learn:

Three copies protect the OS from any loss.

Key concepts:

3 fronts · .gitignore · redundancy.

What is:

Define when context gets stale (laws change in months, not overnight).

Why learn:

Knowing the rate of deterioration keeps you from trusting stale data.

Key concepts:

Degradation · cutoff · update.

What is:

A slash command that saves the summary and updates CLAUDE.md/rules with a human in the loop.

Why learn:

It's the OS's controlled self-improvement over time.

Key concepts:

/tldr · self-improvement · human in the loop.

What is:

Weekly crons that distill all conversations into synthesized nuggets.

Why learn:

Keep the substrate fresh without manual work.

Key concepts:

Cron · synthesis · nuggets.

What is:

A ready-to-use prompt that creates the /tldr post-session summary that updates the OS.

Why learn:

It’s the copy-run that closes the production loop.

Key concepts:

Copy · run · review the summary.
